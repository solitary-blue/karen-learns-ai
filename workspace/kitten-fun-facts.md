# Surprising Kitten Facts

---

## Welcome to the World of Kittens!

Kittens are more than just cute bundles of fur. They have some truly amazing biological features and behaviors that most people never notice.

---

## The "Kindle" of Kittens

Did you know that a group of kittens isn't just called a "litter"?

> [!TIP] A Kindle of Kittens
> While a "litter" refers to siblings born at the same time, any group of unrelated kittens is technically called a **kindle**.

---

## All Blue Everything

Every single kitten is born with blue eyes! 

- Their permanent eye color doesn't start to develop until they are about 6-7 weeks old.
- The blue color comes from a lack of pigment in the iris at birth.

---

## The Sweet Mystery

Kittens (and all cats) are among the few mammals that cannot taste sweetness.

> [!INFO] Taste Bud Trivia
> They lack the DNA for the "sweet" taste receptor. To a kitten, a spoonful of sugar tastes exactly like a spoonful of water!

---

## Whiskers: The Natural Ruler

A kitten's whiskers are generally about as long as the kitten is wide.

- This helps them judge whether they can fit through a tight space.
- If their whiskers touch the sides, they know it's too narrow!

> [!WARNING] Never Trim Whiskers!
> Trimming a kitten's whiskers is like blindfolding them; it disorients them and affects their balance.

---

## The Third Eyelid

Kittens have a "third eyelid" called a **haw** or nictitating membrane.

- It's located in the inner corner of the eye.
- It helps protect the eye and keep it moist without the kitten needing to blink as often as we do.

---

## Purring vs. Roaring

In the feline world, you can either purr or roar, but you can't do both.

> [!EXAMPLE] The Purr-Roar Divide
> Small cats (like kittens) have a rigid hyoid bone that allows them to purr continuously while breathing in and out. Big cats (like lions) have a flexible hyoid that lets them roar but prevents them from purring.

---

## Professional Sleepers

Kittens spend an incredible amount of time sleeping to support their rapid growth.

- A kitten spends about **2/3 of its life** asleep.
- By the time a kitten is 9 years old, it has only been awake for 3 years!
